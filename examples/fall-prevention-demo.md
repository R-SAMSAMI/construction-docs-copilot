# Managing Fall Protection Hazards Workbook

## Purpose

This workbook is intended to help workers and supervisors recognize fall hazards, understand basic fall-prevention practices, and reinforce safe planning before elevated work begins.

## Main Safety Themes

- Workers should identify fall hazards before starting any task performed at height.
- Supervisors should make sure the planned work method is appropriate for the location and exposure.
- Access routes, walking surfaces, and work platforms should be checked before use.
- The crew should understand which protective systems, safe-work practices, and training expectations apply to the task.

## Planning Before Work Starts

- Review the scope of work and identify where workers could be exposed to unprotected edges, openings, or unstable access.
- Confirm who is responsible for supervision, setup, inspection, and communication.
- Make sure the crew understands the task sequence and any restrictions on where they can work.
- Stop and escalate questions when the safe method is unclear.

## Worker Responsibilities

- Follow the approved work plan and site safety rules.
- Use the required protective equipment and follow instructions for the assigned task.
- Report unsafe conditions, damaged equipment, missing controls, or unclear instructions.
- Stay alert to changes in weather, housekeeping, or nearby activity that could increase fall risk.

## Supervisor Responsibilities

- Verify that workers have appropriate instruction for the task.
- Review the work area before the task begins.
- Confirm that access, housekeeping, and work-zone controls are in place.
- Reinforce safe behavior and correct unsafe conditions before work continues.

## Access And Work Areas

- Ladders, platforms, and other access points should be used only as intended.
- Work areas should be kept clear enough to reduce trips, slips, and missteps.
- Materials and tools should be staged so they do not create avoidable hazards near edges or access routes.
- Workers should avoid shortcuts that bypass safe access or stable working positions.

## Inspections And Conditions

- Equipment and work areas should be checked before use.
- Damaged or questionable equipment should be removed from service until it is reviewed.
- Unsafe conditions should be reported immediately.
- Work should pause when changing conditions make the original plan unreliable.

## Training And Communication

- Workers should understand the hazards of elevated work before starting.
- The crew should know who to contact when a condition changes or a requirement is unclear.
- Pre-task communication should cover the work area, expected hazards, and the controls the team will use.
- Toolbox talks and refresher training can be used to reinforce safe habits.
